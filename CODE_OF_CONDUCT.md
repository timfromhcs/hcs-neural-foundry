# Code of conduct

## Our commitment

This project is a technical workspace. Everyone should be able to use it and contribute to it without
being harassed, dismissed, or made to feel unwelcome — regardless of experience level, background,
identity, or which library they happen to prefer.

## Expected behaviour

* **Argue about evidence, not people.** "Your benchmark is not comparable because the budgets differ"
  is good. "You clearly don't understand this" is not.
* **Say what you do not know.** A confident wrong answer costs more here than an honest "not measured".
* **Respect the claim rules.** They exist because this field is full of overstated results; pointing at
  a missing baseline is a kindness, not an attack.
* **Give credit.** Cite the paper, the author and the licence when you build on someone's work.

## Unacceptable behaviour

* Harassment, insults, or discriminatory comments of any kind.
* Publishing someone's private information.
* Deliberately overstating results, hiding failed runs, or tampering with benchmarks.
* Using the issue tracker to push unrelated products or agendas.
* Pressuring maintainers to publish, deploy, or merge something before the gates pass.

## Scope

This applies to issues, pull requests, discussions, commit messages and any space where someone is
representing the project.

## Enforcement

Maintainers may edit or remove contributions that violate this document and may temporarily or
permanently restrict participation. Decisions are explained to the person concerned.

Report concerns privately to the maintainers listed in `pyproject.toml`. Reports are handled
confidentially.
